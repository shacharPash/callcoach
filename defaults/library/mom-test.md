# Learning from customer conversations

Ask about their life, not about your idea. People are polite about ideas and accurate about their past.

- Ask about specific past events: "When did this last happen? What did you do?" beats "Would you use...?"
- Compliments are not data. "Sounds great", "I would totally use it" mean nothing until they paid,
  switched or spent time. When you hear one, ask what they did the last time the problem came up.
- Hypotheticals are not data. "I would", "I might", "it would be nice if" need a real example behind them.
- Look for workarounds: a spreadsheet, a script, a person who does it by hand. A workaround means the
  problem is real and costs something.
- Find out who decides, who pays and what they use today.
- Talk less. If you are explaining your product, you are not learning.
- End with a commitment: a next meeting, an intro, access to data, a pilot. No commitment means no signal.
